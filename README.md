# Every Delivery Room

**Neonatal resuscitation training at national scale and the limits of aggregate evidence.**
El Salvador, 1995-2020.

Between 2000 and 2004 the Ministry of Health of El Salvador trained the perinatal staff of all 28 public delivery
hospitals in the American Academy of Pediatrics Neonatal Resuscitation Program, and kept training them every year
until 2019. Neonatal mortality fell by more than half over the period. This project asks how much of that decline
the training can be shown to explain when the only record is one national series and a start date, and what a
conclusive answer would take.

## What the essay shows

The essay runs in five chapters, each built around a graphic that changes as you scroll, and a closing section.

1. **Four years to train a country.** A workforce of about 4,500 providers, coverage from 5 to 76 percent between 2000 and 2004, and 17,695 trainings by 2019.
2. **The decline, and the trap.** The Ministry's series fell by two thirds; it was already falling seven percent a year before the program and did not accelerate; the sharp break in 2009 is a change in the reporting system, not in risk.
3. **Synthetic El Salvador.** A weighted mix of Latin American countries that reproduces the 1990s path; a 13 percent excess decline over 2000-2019 that placebo tests place within the region's ordinary variation.
4. **Where the roll-out shows up.** First-week deaths fell 26 percent during the roll-out while deaths at 7 to 27 days did not; deaths attributed to birth asphyxia fell three times faster than other causes; the ratio of neonatal to post-neonatal mortality stayed flat where the region's rose.
5. **The region, and the bill.** The largest proportional decline in Latin America, a bounded effect of about half a death per thousand institutional births, and a cost per DALY far below any regional threshold at a fraction of that bound.

**Who was not in the room.** The program was universal inside the public hospitals, on which about four fifths of the population depends, and absent for the births that took place outside a facility during the roll-out. Whether the decline was shared across wealth quintiles and regions is a question the aggregate data cannot answer; the household surveys can.

Every chart has hover values and a "Show the numbers" table. The page is a single self-contained HTML file and embeds aggregates only.

## Methods in brief

Interrupted time series on the administrative series (segmented regression, 1995-2008 window, Newey-West and
Prais-Winsten errors, placebo interruption dates). Synthetic control on UN IGME estimates with 24 Latin American
donors (classic, covariate-based and ridge-augmented estimators; permutation inference; leave-one-out; in-time
placebo; alternative vintages and donor pools). Age-at-death composition and a control outcome (post-neonatal
mortality) within the country and across countries. Cause-specific institutional mortality 1999-2004. Bounds and a
cost-effectiveness scenario grid rather than a point estimate.

## Data

- Ministry of Health of El Salvador: institutional infant, neonatal and early neonatal mortality per 1,000 live
  births (1995/2000-2020) and providers trained in NRP per year (2000-2019), compiled with the Comité Nacional de
  Reanimación Neonatal for the study led by Oswaldo Revelo-Castro with Waldemar A. Carlo and Juan Morales.
- Revelo-Castro et al. (2005), Pediatric Academic Societies: institutional neonatal and asphyxia mortality 1999-2004.
- UN IGME 2021 (via Our World in Data) and 2024 (via Gapminder, childmortality.org); WHO/MCEE cause-of-death
  estimates; World Bank, IHME and WHO covariates (via Gapminder); Natural Earth outlines.

The Ministry series are aggregate national rates; there are no individual records anywhere in the project.
